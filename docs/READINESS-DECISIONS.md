# Planning decisions and implementation-readiness gate

## Confirmed decisions from the questionnaire

These decisions are approved product direction and should be carried into the detailed specification. They do not close the implementation gate or replace the remaining operational, legal, provider, and acceptance details below.

### Product, market, and tenant model

- Product name: **MylesNet**. Serve both single-branch and growing multi-branch ISPs, starting with single-branch operators.
- The initial operating company that contracts with ISP tenants will be incorporated in **Kenya**.
- Use separate retention schedules for subscriber, financial, RADIUS, security/audit, and consent data. MylesNet supplies country- and category-specific defaults and guardrails; tenants may choose shorter periods where lawful. Legal holds override deletion. Retain each category only as long as its purpose or applicable law requires, then delete or anonymize it. Obtain local counsel validation of country-specific periods before launch.
- Use managed Convex **Europe (Ireland)** as the leading v1 data-region candidate. Confirm measured latency from Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania, current pricing, and country-specific transfer safeguards before production; treat this as a candidate rather than a launched-region approval.
- Host Next.js on **Vercel** with server functions in **Dublin (`dub1`)**, aligned with the leading Convex Ireland candidate; serve static assets through Vercel’s global CDN. Confirm the plan, failover, latency, cost, and cross-border processing details before production.
- Measure v1 success across all four outcomes: active paying ISP tenants, subscriber accounts and successful sessions served, RADIUS/captive portal reliability, and subscriber payment processing/reconciliation. Target **1,000 active paying ISP tenants and 100,000 active subscriber accounts by 12 months after public launch**. The subscriber target uses a planning assumption of 100 active accounts per tenant on average; it is a scenario calculation, not a market forecast. For measurement, count distinct, non-test subscriber accounts with a valid service entitlement at month-end; exclude expired, suspended, duplicate, and test accounts. Track both monthly successful session starts and the success rate of eligible login attempts; keep credential, account-state, and policy denials separate from infrastructure failures. Target at least **99.5% monthly success for eligible subscriber login attempts**. Set managed RADIUS endpoint and hosted captive portal availability at **99.95% per month each** (about 22 minutes unavailable in a 30-day month). At least **99% of valid payment confirmations received by MylesNet should activate service within 30 seconds**. At least **99% of provider-confirmed payments should be recorded and reconciled within one minute**; unresolved cases appear immediately in the exception queue. Review the scorecard monthly and reassess target assumptions quarterly; document metric sources and acceptance tests before implementation.
- Launch countries: **Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania**. One tenant may span countries and legal entities. Each branch has its own country and local currency; legal entities keep separate country, tax, invoicing, and currency settings.
- Set each branch’s timezone from its country default, and allow a tenant admin to override it.
- Use **one regional data plane** for all three countries, with documented transfer safeguards and a country-by-country legal launch gate. Before enabling production personal-data processing for a country, obtain local legal review of the relevant controller/processor roles, transfer routes, safeguards, and any permit or notification requirements. This records the architecture posture, not a legal determination.
- Regulatory scoping reviewed 11 November 2026: Kenya’s [Data Protection (General) Regulations, 2021](https://www.odpc.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/THE-DATA-PROTECTION-GENERAL-REGULATIONS-2021-1.pdf) describe conditions for cross-border transfers; Uganda’s [Data Protection and Privacy Act, 2019, section 19](https://ict.go.ug/ims/public/site/documents/Data-Protection-and-Privacy-Act-2019.pdf) addresses processing or storage outside Uganda; Tanzania’s [PDPC cross-border transfer permit guidance](https://pdpc.go.tz/services/cross-border-data-transfer-permit/) describes its permit process. Confirm current applicability and required steps with local counsel before launch.
- Reports must support both per-currency totals and consolidated totals converted using the latest official central-bank reference rate at view time. Show the applied rate and as-of time.
- First release targets the full researched reference scope: tenant modules, platform administration, public marketing, analytics, outreach, and subscriber/captive portals. Include all 308 captured public marketing routes as static or structured pages unless editing is required.
- V1 data migration uses guided, permission-gated CSV imports with server-side validation, duplicate checks within the file and against tenant data, a non-mutating dry-run preview, explicit commit, audit trail, and safe batch-scoped rollback. Do not silently merge or overwrite duplicates. Named system-specific connectors are deferred beyond v1.
- The subscriber account/renewal portal and captive portal use the tenant's approved brand inputs and shared design system; tenant theme controls remain limited to verified tenant-facing surfaces.
- Multi-branch management is in v1. Use a shared Convex deployment with strict tenant and branch isolation; dedicated deployments remain a possible future isolation option after workload, security, legal, and cost review, not an included offer.
- New tenant signup is self-service with automatic provisioning after the first Owner verifies their email; a phone number may be added and verified later.
- Do not collect a government-issued business registration ID during signup in v1.
- Allow the same business name on different tenant signups when each has a distinct verified Owner identity and unique subdomain. Block a repeated Owner identity attempting to own another tenant, and block duplicate subdomains.
- If automatic provisioning fails, retry transient steps idempotently, preserve signup progress, show the current status, and provide a support path after a bounded retry threshold. Set exact retry/backoff and escalation targets in the implementation runbook.
- A person/account may own at most one ISP tenant. A verified staff identity may also belong to multiple tenant organizations; tenant switching is explicit and clears the previous tenant's data before showing the selected tenant.
- The tenant's default MylesNet subdomain is stable after signup, but the Owner may request a verified rename later. Keep the previous subdomain reserved as a redirect alias; never reassign it to another tenant. The tenant display name can change independently.
- Tenant Owners and Admins may invite staff and assign initial role/branch scope. Admins remain within their permission ceiling; Owner approval is required for high-risk grants. Ordinary invitations cannot assign the Owner role; use the separate ownership-transfer flow. Invitations are single-use and expire after **7 days**.
- Built-in roles cannot be deleted; the Tenant Owner may customize permissions for built-in roles other than Owner. The Owner or an Admin with role-management permission may delete a custom role only when no staff are assigned; Admins remain within their permission ceiling. Preserve the deleted role's history in the audit trail.
- The Owner or a tenant-wide Admin may deactivate a non-Owner staff member. A branch-scoped Admin may revoke that member's access only within the Admin's assigned branches. The Owner cannot be deactivated through ordinary staff management; use the ownership-transfer process.
- Tenant authorization includes built-in staff roles **Owner, Admin, Finance, Network Operator, Support Agent, Sales/Agent, and Analyst/Read-only**, plus tenant-composed custom roles/permissions. The Owner role is fixed; the Tenant Owner may edit permission bundles for the other built-in roles within that tenant. Admin may compose custom roles within their own permission ceiling. Custom roles may be built from scratch or cloned from a role as an independent permission set, with no live inheritance. The subscriber portal uses a separate account type and authorization model. Each tenant has **exactly one active Owner**, who is always tenant-wide; Admin and all functional/custom roles may be assigned tenant-wide or to selected branches. A staff member has one role per assignment scope and may combine one tenant-wide assignment with branch-specific assignments. A tenant-wide role applies to all branches; branch roles add permissions only within their assigned branches. Tenants compose a custom role to combine duties within a scope. The Tenant Owner must grant or approve these high-risk permissions: withdrawals/payout destinations, refunds/credits, sensitive exports, credential/secret reveal or rotation, role delegation, bulk messaging, and destructive/bulk network actions. Admin may compose other custom roles but cannot self-escalate.
- To transfer ownership, the current Owner initiates and completes MFA step-up; the incoming Owner verifies identity, accepts, and completes MFA step-up (enrolling if needed) before the transfer takes effect. The former Owner becomes a tenant-wide Admin by default; the incoming Owner may choose a narrower role or remove the former Owner's access during acceptance. Notify verified tenant contacts. If the current Owner is unavailable, use the established owner-recovery process.
- Removing or deactivating a tenant staff member takes effect immediately: revoke their membership and active sessions, stop live data subscriptions, and clear tenant-scoped client data. Preserve an audit record.
- Role, permission, and branch-scope changes take effect immediately: recompute access, end subscriptions and clear data the staff member is no longer allowed to see, while keeping their sign-in session active.
- Built-in MylesNet platform roles are **Owner, Operations, Finance, Support, Security Admin, and Auditor**. Platform administrators use MFA; Security Admin independently approves high-risk owner account recovery.
- Platform administrators must use MFA. Tenant staff MFA enrollment is optional at sign-in, but high-risk actions require MFA step-up; staff without an enrolled factor cannot perform those actions. Subscriber accounts use email or phone plus password, with MFA for sensitive actions. Enterprise SSO and SCIM are deferred beyond v1.
- WorkOS AuthKit sends identity-flow emails (invitations, email verification, password resets, and Magic Auth codes) using WorkOS’s default sender. WorkOS owns identity-flow logic and code/token generation. Resend is reserved for MylesNet application emails such as withdrawal codes, service notices, and invoices.
- Tenant-owner account recovery uses verified email/phone self-service. If all verified methods are lost, trained support verifies current business status and claimant identity/authority, then calls a trusted tenant contact already on file. Apply a **24-hour default cooling-off period**, extended to **72 hours for high-risk cases**. If no trusted contact can be reached, treat recovery as high risk: require a separate MFA-protected **Platform Security Admin**, distinct from the support initiator, as second approver; require a current official business-register record or formal company resolution and a live check of the claimant’s government ID; the second approver independently verifies the evidence. Notify all previously verified owner contacts when a request opens and when a final decision is made, include a dispute path, and never use newly supplied contacts for these notices. Record an audit trail; this recovery does not grant support routine tenant-data access.
- On voluntary cancellation, the tenant console and subscriber network service remain available through the paid billing period; send advance warnings and provide a tenant data-export window before service ends.
- After cancellation takes effect, tenant administrators have a **30-day data-export window**. Once it ends, begin deletion according to each data category’s lawful retention schedule, subject to legal holds and mandatory retention.
- A tenant may reactivate during the 30-day export window after payment and verification; restore service without requiring archive restoration. At the scheduled cancellation effective time, stop tenant console and network services, including active subscriber sessions.
- Send tenant cancellation and service-end reminders **30 days, 7 days, and 1 day** before the paid period ends.
- Platform support has no routine tenant-data access. Standard access is read-only and requires the Tenant Owner’s approval of a time-limited, scoped grant, with a visible banner and audit trail. The Tenant Owner may explicitly allow internal support notes and corrections to non-sensitive contact/profile fields only. Support grants exclude finance, credentials, roles, network/router actions, messaging, exports, deletion, and suspension. Each grant lasts at most **one hour**; a new approval is required to extend it. For documented emergency exceptions, a Platform Security Admin and a different Platform Security Admin or Platform Owner must approve; self-approval is prohibited, the tenant is notified when access begins, and emergency grants remain read-only.
- Tenant support coverage is **Monday–Friday, 08:00–17:00 EAT, excluding public holidays**, with **24/7 on-call** for a MylesNet-managed tenant-wide or multi-tenant access/captive-portal outage, a broad payment-processing outage, or a major security incident. Customer-side router, power, and uplink faults do not trigger MylesNet’s critical on-call path. The on-call network/platform engineer is incident commander; support coordinates tenant updates. Acknowledge P1 incidents and begin triage within **15 minutes**; provide tenant updates every **30 minutes** until service is restored or the incident is downgraded. For MylesNet-controlled P1s, target minimum service restoration or a safe workaround within **60 minutes** as an internal objective, subject to architecture validation before any customer-facing promise.

### SaaS billing and lifecycle

- Standard self-service pricing offers **Hotspot** and **PPPoE** modes. They share the standard platform feature set, and one tenant may use either or both. **Enterprise** is a custom-quoted offer for an ISP with at least **10,000 active subscribers** or a documented multi-region/regulatory requirement. No hard subscriber/router caps, branch/entity caps, per-staff charges, or per-voucher charges apply to the standard models; retain operational abuse safeguards. Enterprise may include dedicated onboarding/account management, migration help, custom integrations/API/branding, and negotiated contract/support terms. Dedicated infrastructure or stronger guarantees require separate validation.
- Hotspot usage is **3%** of audited, confirmed Hotspot sales before payment-provider fees, less refunds and reversals; exclude pending or failed payments. Include confirmed cash, bank, manual mobile-money, and provider-confirmed sales under both tenant-owned and MylesNet-managed collection models. PPPoE usage is **USD $0.25 reference per distinct non-test account per month** when a valid PPPoE entitlement was active at any point in the billing month; count it once at the full unit without proration. Publish fixed, versioned local-currency rates for KES, UGX, and TZS after validating the equivalents.
- Standard usage billing is monthly, with no standard annual-prepayment discount. Enterprise may negotiate annual commitments. Apply one **$5-equivalent monthly minimum** to the combined Hotspot and PPPoE charge in every paid billing month, including a zero-usage month. Validate fixed local-currency minimums for KES, UGX, and TZS before launch. Calculate Hotspot fees in each sale currency; convert them to the selected invoice currency with the official central-bank reference rate at invoice issuance and show/lock the applied rate and as-of time. Rates are before tax; list applicable taxes separately per billing entity. MylesNet absorbs payment-processing costs for its own subscription invoices.
- The public pricing calculator is anonymous; visitors enter monthly Hotspot revenue and billable PPPoE account estimates, and see the combined monthly minimum before tax. Select KES/UGX/TZS through coarse IP-based country lookup for the three launch markets; fall back to USD if lookup fails or the visitor is elsewhere. Keep the manual selector visible, do not request GPS, and do not store precise location. Enterprise quote form fields are company name, work email, countries served, estimated subscriber count, Hotspot/PPPoE usage, and a short needs summary; never ask for credentials or registration IDs.
- Give the Owner at least **30 days' notice** before changing a published usage rate; apply it only from a future billing cycle, never retroactively. Exact local PPPoE rates and minimums, tax treatment, and negotiated Enterprise prices remain open pending cost, country-market, provider, and legal validation.
- The self-service trial lasts **14 days**.
- Start the 14-day trial after the first Owner verifies email; do not require a payment method upfront. Before any first charge, require billing setup and explicit confirmation by the tenant. Detailed verification-failure, duplicate-identity, and partial-provisioning recovery behavior remains open in the [MN-PLAN-026 decision packet](ISP-SIGNUP-PROVISIONING-RECOVERY-DECISION-PACKET.md); that packet does not change the approved trial or billing rules.
- After the Tenant Owner explicitly confirms the subscription, standard usage billing renews monthly until the Owner cancels. The first successful paid invoice sets the billing anchor; invoice dates recur monthly, using the cycle's last day if the anchor day is absent and returning to the original anchor date in the next cycle. Generate an invoice each period. Mobile money, bank transfer, and card invoices require the tenant to select **Pay**; only separately enabled wallet auto-pay charges automatically. The Owner may cancel before the next renewal; service continues through the paid period. Enterprise billing cadence and contract terms may be negotiated.
- No standard tier upgrade/downgrade or subscriber/router quota transition applies. A change in metered usage affects the next applicable invoice under the published versioned rates.
- If the tenant has not completed billing setup and confirmation by trial end, provide a **7-day completion grace period** with console and network service continuing; then apply the established unpaid-tenant suspension rules.
- Usage is accumulated across all branches/legal entities for the tenant's single subscription invoice. A selected billing legal entity determines the invoice currency; cross-currency Hotspot fee conversion uses the invoice-issuance rule above. Branch and legal-entity count are not plan-limited.
- Failed platform payments have a **7-day grace period**. Afterward, suspend the tenant console and network service, reject new RADIUS logins, and disconnect active subscriber sessions until payment.
- A tenant has one MylesNet subscription across its entities and branches. The tenant selects one billing legal entity, and the MylesNet invoice uses that entity’s local currency.
- Accept local mobile money and bank transfer for MylesNet subscription invoices, plus card payment where a provider supports it. Collect these through MylesNet's own provider merchant accounts, separate from ISP subscriber collections; reuse provider integrations where possible. A tenant collecting subscriber money through MylesNet may opt in to wallet auto-pay; keep it off by default. Only the Tenant Owner may enable or disable it, with MFA step-up. For approved pooled-collection countries, charge the wallet for the selected billing legal entity one day before the subscription invoice is due and retry hourly while the balance is insufficient, until the invoice is covered. Use only the wallet in the exact invoice currency; never convert or draw from another legal entity. Tenants using direct-to-merchant collections do not have MylesNet wallet auto-pay.
- If the tenant is suspended after the seven-day grace while wallet auto-pay remains enabled, continue hourly retries; after the overdue invoice is fully paid, automatically restore the tenant console and network service.
- If the Owner disables wallet auto-pay while an invoice is unpaid, cancel all future wallet attempts immediately. Keep any completed payment; the remaining invoice stays payable through manual methods.
- Send reminders for unpaid MylesNet subscription invoices **7, 3, and 1 day** before the due date; stop reminders as soon as payment is recorded.

### RADIUS, routers, and access plans

- Support both PPPoE and hotspot/captive portal with vouchers in v1. MikroTik RouterOS is the first-class NAS integration; other standard RADIUS-compatible devices may use core RADIUS features, with vendor-specific integrations later.
- MylesNet runs a redundant regional RADIUS service. Use FreeRADIUS 3.2.x with a dedicated PostgreSQL AAA store; keep RADIUS authentication/accounting off Convex’s request path while Convex remains the SaaS and realtime application backend.
- Every MikroTik router must use an operator-pasted provisioning command to establish a WireGuard tunnel and requires compatible RouterOS 7. The tunnel permits RADIUS authentication/accounting and CoA/Disconnect session control only; block WinBox, SSH, and RouterOS API access.
- If a branch loses RADIUS connectivity, existing sessions continue and new logins are rejected until service returns.
- Subscriber plans support tenant-configurable combinations of speed, data quota, validity/time, session limits, and fair-use rules. When a subscriber plan expires, reject new logins and immediately disconnect active sessions until renewal/payment.
- FUP is configured per package. Tenants choose throttle or disconnect/block, with a notice when the limit is reached. The usage-reset options are each purchase, daily, weekly, or monthly.
- Share package templates across a tenant, with branch-specific local price, currency, and availability.

### Payments, payouts, and communications

- Support both subscriber collection models: a tenant’s own provider account or MylesNet-managed collection. Each tenant chooses during onboarding.
- Account for every provider-confirmed subscriber payment exactly once. Route unresolved or ambiguous outcomes to a visible review queue immediately while safe retries continue; do not silently drop events or create duplicate credits.
- Allow tenant staff with the appropriate permission to record cash, bank-transfer, and manual mobile-money subscriber payments with a payment reference and audit trail. One authorized staff member may immediately credit the subscriber and activate service after recording the payment.
- Support full and partial subscriber refunds through MylesNet when the selected gateway supports them; otherwise create a visible provider/manual exception for follow-up and reconciliation.
- Let each tenant configure refund approval: a second approver for every refund, only above a tenant-set amount, or no second approver. Refund initiators still require the tenant’s scoped permission and all actions remain audited.
- A full refund of an active package cancels that package and immediately disconnects the subscriber’s active sessions; retain the subscriber account. A partial refund changes the financial ledger only; leave package and access unchanged unless a separate service action is taken.
- For MylesNet-managed collection, funds settle to MylesNet and MylesNet pays the tenant out. Keep this flow disabled per country until local legal and provider approval is confirmed.
- Pooled payouts are tenant-initiated and submitted immediately after verification. Use the tenant’s saved, verified payout destination for the legal entity; the owner approves destination additions or changes.
- Every withdrawal requires a one-time code sent to the current Tenant Owner’s verified email. This recipient changes when ownership transfers. The Owner or a user with a dedicated branch-scoped withdrawal permission may initiate it.
- **Resend** is the leading v1 transactional email provider candidate for MylesNet application messages, but use it only after country-specific legal/contract approval of US data processing and East African inbox delivery tests. Minimize email content; use Amazon SES in Ireland if Resend is not approved for a launch country.
- Do not impose MylesNet withdrawal caps beyond the available balance; provider/network limits still apply. Quote separate platform and provider fees per country and payout rail, and show the fee breakdown and recipient net before confirmation. Platform fees may be fixed, percentage-based, or both.
- On confirmed payout failure, restore the principal automatically, retain the failed ledger entry, and require a new request for retry.
- Subscriber payment-provider direction: **iOtec for Uganda; Safaricom Daraja for Kenya; ClickPesa first and Selcom backup for Tanzania**, subject to provider/account validation. ClickPesa’s public payout documentation currently states a 60-second gap between create requests per merchant; confirm its impact on pooled volume and instant withdrawals before enabling it.
- Keep the SMS provider unselected pending quotes and live delivery results. Include **Africa’s Talking and Infobip** in the quote and handset-delivery pilot for Kenya, Uganda, and Tanzania; keep Twilio as a pricing benchmark only. Use a tenant’s registered brand sender ID where approved, otherwise a configured fallback. Promotional SMS requires separate opt-in and unsubscribe/STOP suppression; service notifications remain separate.
- Subscriber-facing pages and captive portal support English and Kiswahili, with a tenant default and customer language switch.

- Treat the supplied saved product screenshots as workflow/layout references only. Do not copy real subscriber names, phone numbers, network identifiers, or credentials into MylesNet artifacts.
